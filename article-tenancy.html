Understanding Tenancy Rights in Nigeria: A Practical Guide for Tenants and Landlords

Updated October 2026 • 6 min read

1. Statutory Notices Required Before Eviction

Under Nigerian property law (including the Recovery of Premises Laws applicable in various states), a landlord cannot arbitrarily eject a tenant without serving statutory notices:

  • Yearly Tenant: Entitled to a 6-month Notice to Quit.
  • Half-Yearly Tenant: Entitled to a 3-month Notice to Quit.
  • Quarterly Tenant: Entitled to a 3-month Notice to Quit.
  • Monthly Tenant: Entitled to a 1-month Notice to Quit.
  • Weekly Tenant: Entitled to a 1-week Notice to Quit.

2. Notice of Owner's Intention to Apply to Recover Possession

Upon the expiration of a valid Notice to Quit, if the tenant remains in possession, the landlord must serve a statutory 7-Day Notice of Owner's Intention to Apply to Recover Possession before instituting an action in court.

3. Protection Against Self-Help and Forcible Ejection

It is unlawful in Nigeria for a landlord to resort to self-help—such as removing roof sheets, locking gates, removing doors, or disconnecting electricity—to forcefully eject a tenant without a valid court order.
