Understanding Tenancy Rights in Nigeria: Notice Periods & Tenant Protection

Published by LegalHub NG | Reviewed under Recovery of Premises Laws

1. Introduction

Tenancy disputes are among the most common legal issues faced by individuals and businesses in Nigeria. Whether renting a residential apartment or commercial space, understanding tenant rights and landlord obligations under Nigerian property law is essential.

2. Statutory Notice Periods for Eviction

Under the Recovery of Premises Act (and regional state tenancy laws like the Lagos State Tenancy Law 2011), a landlord cannot forcefully evict a tenant without serving appropriate legal notice. Statutory notice durations depend on the nature of tenancy:

  • Weekly Tenancy: 7 days' notice (1 week)
  • Monthly Tenancy: 1 month's notice
  • Quarterly / Half-Yearly Tenancy: 3 months' notice
  • Yearly Tenancy: 6 months' notice

Note: Notice periods must be issued in writing and served properly to be legally valid.

3. Notice of Owner's Intention to Apply to Recover Possession

After the expiration of the initial statutory Notice to Quit, if the tenant remains in possession, the landlord must serve a mandatory 7-Day Notice of Owner's Intention to Apply to Recover Possession before filing an action in court.

4. Unlawful Eviction & Self-Help

It is illegal in Nigeria for a landlord to resort to self-help tactics, such as:

  • Removing doors or windows
  • Disconnecting electricity or water supply
  • Locking out tenants or throwing out belongings

Landlords who engage in self-help can be sued for trespass and unlawful ejection, entitling the tenant to damages.

5. Key Tenant Rights

  • Right to Quiet and Peaceful Enjoyment: Tenants are entitled to undisturbed use of the property.
  • Right to Written Rent Receipt: Landlords are legally bound to issue written receipts upon payment.
  • Right to Fair Notice: Landlords must strictly follow lawful eviction procedures.
